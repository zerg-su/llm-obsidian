## Review finding disposition

The first real RC1 review found two critical product defects and three unique
evidence defects (the baseline issue appeared on both axes). RC1 fixed the
stale terminal-approval path, removed synthetic Stop creation, bound the
baseline to its real commit, replaced the 2.6.4 fixture with RC1 dogfood, and
moved final evidence outside the product commit sequence. All six callback
finding IDs are classified in `v2.6.6-rc1-findings.json`.

The final exact-HEAD review then found four evidence/authority gaps. RC1 removed
the unreachable positive `turn-stopped` path, archived the original dogfood
bytes, declared the A-G receipt ledger retrospective rather than independent,
and durably amended the candidate ceiling for one final closure cycle. Those
four IDs are also classified in `v2.6.6-rc1-findings.json`.

That closure review approved intent but found one material E4 handoff gap:
the normalized dispatch request could carry a re-resolved branch SHA different
from the sealed manifest parent. The user retired the stale 2.6.4 goal and
authorized one E4-only closure cycle. Split now rejects that mismatch before
pipeline compilation, child worktree creation or provider launch, with direct
and moved-branch public-facade regressions. The finding is classified as
`ENG.SPLIT.BASE_REQUEST_DRIFT`.

The next bounded review approved intent and exposed one replay regression in
that repair: a valid raw request may omit the derived `base_sha`, so an already
accepted child was rejected while reconstructing its binding. The user
authorized one narrow replay closure. Launched children now recover the sealed
base from their immutable launch receipt; not-yet-launched children retain the
strict normalized-request check. Public replay tests prove zero duplicate
dispatch/provider/worktree/surface effects and reject receipt/manifest base
drift. The finding is classified as
`ENG.SPLIT.REPLAY.BASE_SHA_MISSING`.

The consolidated Codex and Opus review then exposed a public receipt omission,
an unsafe recovery assumption, a weak replay fixture, a stale candidate ledger
and the already-declared E6 proof limitation. RC1 now serializes `base_sha`,
derives recovery authority only from the actual durable child contract, tests a
schema-valid request after its mutable source ref is removed, keeps one
duplicate-receipt authority, records the complete superseded-candidate ledger,
and binds the typed E6 accepted deviation into the final release review.

The dogfood also exposed one adjacent lifecycle defect: accepted callbacks did
not automatically enter the review gate during dispatch finalization. The
callbacks remain immutable, supported close cleared every owned resource, and
no effect was replayed. This is therefore recorded as
`RC2.REVIEW_CALLBACK_INGESTION_FINALIZING`, not silently repaired in RC1.

## Release boundary

RC1 is technically ready only when the sidecar gate, release evidence map and
exact-HEAD review are all green and mutually bound for the final commit. The
user-authorized consolidated closure is singular; any further material finding
stops the RC instead of opening another loop. No
push, tag, publish, deploy, installation or merge is authorized by this
readiness record.
